Key Findings
Products
Fruits and Vegetables recorded the highest total sales (178,124.081), while Seafood recorded the lowest (9,077.870).
Household products had the highest average sales per record (149.425), while Baking Goods had the lowest (126.381).
Low Fat products generated higher total sales (776,319.688) than Regular products (425,361.804).
Total sales reflect overall contribution, whereas average sales per record indicate the average sales value for each record.
Outlets
Outlet type: Supermarket Type1 had the highest total sales (787,549.893), while Supermarket Type2 had the highest average sales per record (141.679).
Outlet size: Medium outlets recorded the highest total sales (507,895.736), while High-sized outlets had the highest average sales (142.037).
Location tier: Tier 3 recorded the highest total sales (472,133.033), while Tier 2 had the highest average sales (141.167).
Differences in total sales may be influenced by the number of records in each group.

Limitations
The raw dataset was not provided for data quality verification.
Detailed relationships between sales, visibility, weight and ratings cannot be confirmed from the available summaries.

Profit margins, stock-outs and promotional information were not supplied.
Recommendations
Prioritize inventory planning for Fruits and Vegetables and Snack Foods while monitoring stock-outs and profit margins.
Investigate low-selling categories such as Seafood before reducing their assortment.
Evaluate outlet performance using both total sales and average sales per record.
Test product visibility and placement changes to determine whether they improve sales.
Track customer ratings and sales over time to understand customer preferences.
Check missing values, duplicates, category labels and unusual values before making final business decisions.
Conclusion
Fruits and Vegetables is the leading product category by total sales.
Supermarket Type1 is the strongest outlet type by total sales.
Medium outlets and Tier 3 locations lead their respective groups in total sales.
Average sales comparisons reveal different performance patterns from total sales.
These findings can support better product selection, inventory planning and outlet evaluation, while further data quality checks and analysis are needed to improve decision-making.
